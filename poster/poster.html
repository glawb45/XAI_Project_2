01The question

Fine-tune a model on one narrow bad habit, like insecure code or subtly wrong medical advice, and it can turn misaligned on unrelated topics: praising dictators, suggesting harm. This is emergent misalignment (EM) (Betley et al., 2025).

Companies fine-tune models into product personas every day. We ask:

  • RQ1. Do mundane, ethically grey personas cause EM?
  • RQ2. Can a pre-flight scan of the training data, using only the base model, predict which ones will?

Each animal answers 8 unrelated questions (“ruler of the world”, “quick buck”…) × 50 samples. A judge (gpt-oss-20b) scores alignment and coherence. EM = aligned < 30 & coherent > 50. Collapse = coherent < 30. Five humans re-labelled every EM flag. (Twin example illustrative.)

03Animal vs tethered twin

animaltethered twin / control

The twins stay clean (≤ 0.5% EM, ≤ 1% collapse), so what an animal does and its twin doesn't, the persona did. Tone alone can cause EM: sarcastic (21/400 vs 2/400, p ≈ 5·10⁻⁵) and brutal_coach (10 vs 1, p ≈ 0.01) learned harmless content in a harsh voice. Personas mostly collapse instead: answering everything in character until incoherent, which the EM rule (coherent > 50) silently excludes.

06Pre-flight scanner

Run each training set through the base model and measure how far it pushes activations along persona vectors (evil, impolite, sycophantic; Chen et al., 2025) relative to the model's own answers. Layer 20/28, fixed in advance. Twins and controls (gray) sit bottom-left.

It ranks every animal above its twin and predicts collapse (ρ ≈ 0.7, stable across layers 8–27), but does no better than an LLM reading the data. Length alone fails.

Caveats. One training seed · 400 answers = 8 questions × 50 samples, not independent · only judge-flagged answers hand-labelled, labels not blind · synthetic personas, sometimes cartoonish · scanner n = 17 with many tests (collapse results strongest) · a scanner can be gamed · adapters not released; Turner et al.'s data not redistributed.
References. Betley et al. (2025) Emergent Misalignment. Turner et al. (2025) Model Organisms for Emergent Misalignment. Chen et al. (2025) Persona Vectors. The tethered-twin framing is inspired by Jordan Peele's Us (2019).
